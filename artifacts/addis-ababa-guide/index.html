01 / Find your bearings

A city of layers.

Start with the places that open a window into Addis—from ancient human history to the lively pulse of daily life.

01

National Museum of Ethiopia

Meet Lucy, known in Ethiopia as Dinknesh, and trace humanity’s story at one of the country’s most important museums.

02

Holy Trinity Cathedral

A striking place of worship and remembrance; the cathedral is the final resting place of Haile Selassie.

03

Mount Entoto

Head uphill for fresh air, sweeping city views, and historic churches among the eucalyptus.

04

Merkato

Follow the sounds and colors through one of Africa’s largest open-air markets. Go with time, curiosity, and a sense of direction.

05

Ethnological Museum

Explore Ethiopia’s diverse cultures inside the former palace grounds at Addis Ababa University.

06

Unity Park

A welcoming introduction to Ethiopia’s heritage in the grounds of the Grand Palace, with history, gardens, and more.

A gentle first-day plan

Keep your first outing close by and unhurried. The altitude is real; the city will still be here tomorrow.

02 / Pull up a chair

Good things
are shared.

Meals here are an invitation to slow down. Tear off a piece of injera, reach for a favorite, and let someone show you how it’s done.

FOOD & COFFEE

Injera & wot

Spongy, tangy injera is both plate and utensil. Try richly spiced doro wot, or shiro—a comforting chickpea stew.

Kitfo & tibs

For a different kind of feast, look for kitfo, finely minced and seasoned, or tibs, sizzling pieces of sautéed meat.

Coffee, from its home

Ethiopia is coffee’s birthplace. A coffee ceremony is more than a drink: stay for the roasting, the scent, and the conversation.

Fasting food & beyaynetu

On Wednesdays and Fridays, many observe fasting with plant-based meals. A beyaynetu brings a colorful variety of dishes together on injera.

Tej & gursha

Sample tej, Ethiopia’s honey wine. And if someone offers you a gursha—a bite placed by hand—it’s a warm gesture of care.

03 / A different rhythm

Time, sound & celebration.

Some of the best travel moments come when you let local rhythms set the pace. Here are a few to listen for.

A calendar of its own

Thirteen months

Ethiopia’s calendar has 13 months. Ethiopian time is counted from sunrise, so it may not match the clock you’re used to—double-check plans together.

Listen closely

Eskista & Ethio-jazz

Watch for the expressive shoulder movements of eskista, and make room in your playlist for the unmistakable sound of Ethio-jazz.

September

Meskel

Meskel, the Finding of the True Cross, fills September with ceremony and community celebrations around the great bonfire.

January

Timkat

Timkat marks Epiphany with vibrant processions and water blessings. Check local dates and be a respectful observer.

A global crossroads

African Union

Addis Ababa is home to the African Union headquarters—a diplomatic city with a place in continent-wide conversations.

04 / Before you set out

A little know-how goes a long way.

Bring an open mind, leave space in the day, and use these notes to make your first visit feel easier.

  1. Acclimatize on day one. Addis sits around 2,400 metres above sea level. Take it easy, drink water, and give yourself time to adjust.
  2. Plan for October–February. These months are generally a lovely time to explore; pack layers for cooler highland evenings.
  3. Choose your ride. Ride-hailing, taxis, and the light rail are all options. Allow extra time for traffic and confirm a taxi fare before setting off.
  4. Dress modestly at churches. Cover shoulders and knees, follow posted guidance, and remove shoes where requested.
  5. Ask before photographing people. A simple request makes for a more respectful encounter; accept a no graciously.
  6. Learn two small words. Selam (hello) and Ameseginalehu (thank you) are a good place to begin.
  7. Carry some cash. Birr is useful for smaller purchases and places that may not accept cards. Keep it secure and exchange through trusted sources.
